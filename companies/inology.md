# Inology

> One configurable SaaS version with parameters and APIs beats custom enterprise forks — PM sits in pre-sales to say no to one-offs.

- Stage: enterprise
- Practices: enterprise, discovery, delivery, roles

## Eric Llonch

### Operating loop

- The company's operating loop for enterprise clients involves:
- Understanding unique client workflows and adapting the product through extensive parameterization.
- Navigating long sales cycles and complex integration requirements.
- Leveraging a standardized, single-version product architecture.
- Focusing on adaptability and integration capabilities to meet enterprise demands.

### Ownership

- **Discovery:** Primarily driven by client interactions and sales input, especially for enterprise deals.
- **Prioritization:** A shared responsibility, with product management balancing client requests against the core product strategy.
- **Shipping:** Managed by the development team, with a focus on maintaining a single, adaptable product version.
- **Sales-input:** Integral to product development, particularly for large enterprise deals where sales often drives feature requests.
- **Design:** While important, it is secondary to the adaptability and workflow integration required by enterprise clients.

### Evidence

- *The most difficult thing, I think, is not the product itself, but the sale, closing a large client compared to a small one.*
- *We have a single version for everyone. Everyone has to have the same version with active and inactive parameters that completely change the way of working, but the same version.*
