# NVIDIA

> NVIDIA’s product bet is CUDA + GPU as a locked-in AI platform — software compounds the silicon, and data-center mix now dominates over gaming cards.

- Stage: enterprise
- Practices: enterprise, ai_product, discovery, delivery

## Toni Franquès

### Operating loop

- The core operating loop involves:
- 1.  **Architectural Design:** Designing new GPU architectures and chip functionalities.
- 2.  **Simulation & Modeling:** Using C++ simulators and RTL to model and test chip performance and energy consumption before physical fabrication.
- 3.  **Fabrication:** Sending designs to foundries like TSMC for manufacturing.
- 4.  **Software Development (CUDA):** Creating and refining CUDA to enable efficient programming of GPUs for parallel processing tasks, particularly for AI and scientific computing.
- 5.  **AI Model Integration:** Adapting hardware and software to support the demands of evolving AI models, such as large language models.
- 6.  **Market Feedback & Iteration:** Incorporating feedback on performance, energy efficiency, and new AI requirements to inform the next generation of chip designs.

### Ownership

- **Discovery:** Primarily driven by architectural research and development, informed by market trends in AI and computing.
- **Prioritization:** Guided by the demand for AI/HPC solutions and the need to support evolving AI models and algorithms.
- **Shipping:** Managed through a cadence of new chip releases, often annually or bi-annually, enabled by advancements from manufacturing partners like TSMC and ASML.
- **Sales Input:** Heavily influenced by enterprise demand for data center GPUs and AI-specific hardware.
- **Design:** Led by NVIDIA's internal architecture teams, with significant input from software developers and AI researchers.

### Evidence

- *The proportion of chips sold for data centers has exploded compared to graphics cards.*
- *The more you buy, the more you save, because every year new chips come out.*
