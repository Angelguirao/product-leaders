# Devengo

> Devengo killed an undifferentiated salary-advance product and rebuilt as account-to-account payments infrastructure — the wedge was rails, not consumer UX.

- Stage: growth
- Practices: enterprise, discovery, delivery, growth

## Fernando Cabello

### Operating loop

- The pivot involved a strategic shift from a direct-to-consumer/employee product to a business-to-business (B2B) infrastructure play. This transition required significant product development to build a robust API capable of handling real-time, automated account-to-account payments. The company focused on creating a foundational payment infrastructure that could serve diverse use cases, such as lending, insurtech reimbursements, and e-commerce payouts, as well as enabling other businesses to build payment solutions.

### Ownership

- **Discovery:** The initial product discovery for salary advance was driven by an idea that resonated emotionally. Post-pivot, discovery seems to be more focused on identifying infrastructure needs and potential use cases for their API.
- **Prioritization:** The decision to pivot was driven by market analysis and a strategic assessment of competitive advantages, indicating a shift towards prioritizing market positioning and differentiation.
- **Shipping:** The company successfully launched its new API product within six months of deciding to pivot, demonstrating a strong delivery capability.
- **Sales-input:** While not explicitly detailed, the pivot suggests a move towards understanding and serving B2B clients' infrastructure needs.
- **Design:** The company invested in a new design system, with a focus on creating a new language and potential for long-term continuity.

### Evidence

- *The decision of not pivoting would have been dying slowly or dying quickly, but it would have been dying.*
