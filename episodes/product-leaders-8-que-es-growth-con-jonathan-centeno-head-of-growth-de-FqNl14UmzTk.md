## Thesis
Factorial growth runs a fast acquire→activate experiment loop: ship imperfect tests, learn from activation, cut losers quickly.


## Context
Factorial is an HR software company that aims to streamline administrative tasks and provide data-driven decision-making tools for HR departments. The company has experienced significant growth, expanding from 15 to 1,000 employees.

## Operating loop
The growth team focuses on rapid iteration and validation, exemplified by their approach to launching new features and experiments. This involves quickly building Minimum Viable Products (MVPs), launching them to gather immediate feedback, and then iterating based on whether they succeed or fail. This contrasts with the more complex and slower development cycles in core product areas, which must account for intricate technical dependencies and existing systems.

## Ownership
- **Discovery:** Primarily driven by the growth team's experimentation and market feedback, with input from sales and customer insights.
- **Prioritization:** Focused on hypotheses that can be validated quickly with minimal resources, scaling those that show traction.
- **Shipping:** Emphasizes rapid deployment of MVPs to test assumptions and gather real-world data.
- **Sales Input:** Sales teams provide crucial insights into customer problems and market realities, informing product development and marketing efforts.
- **Design:** Integrated early in the process, with product, design, and engineering collaborating from the outset.

## Evidence
- *The market is not waiting for you. You have to go to the market and reach it.*
- *The best thing you can have is to be able to do that theory and then implement it and know if it works or not.*

## Gaps
- The interview does not detail the specific metrics used to evaluate the success of growth experiments beyond general traction.
- The long-term strategy for scaling the growth team's rapid iteration model into more complex product areas is not fully elaborated.
- The specific challenges and solutions for integrating product marketing efforts with the growth team's experimental approach could be further detailed.
