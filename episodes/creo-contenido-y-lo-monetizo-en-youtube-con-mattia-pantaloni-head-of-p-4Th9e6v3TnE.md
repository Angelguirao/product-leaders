## Thesis
The Power productizes accessible online MBAs — content + YouTube distribution undercut traditional business-school price points.


## Context
The Power is an online education company that initially gained traction with its MBA program, aiming to make business education more affordable and practical. It has since expanded into various other fields, including tech, sports, and pharma, positioning itself as a broad educational institution rather than solely a business school.

## Operating loop
The Power's operating loop involves:
1. Identifying market demand for specific educational programs.
2. Partnering with or hiring subject matter experts to develop curriculum.
3. Leveraging internal expertise in marketing and content delivery to create engaging, concise learning experiences.
4. Utilizing a proprietary platform for content delivery and community building.
5. Iteratively refining programs based on market feedback and evolving educational trends.

## Ownership
- **Discovery:** Primarily driven by market demand and the identification of suitable experts for new program areas.
- **Prioritization:** A mix of market potential, the availability of expert talent, and strategic expansion into new verticals.
- **Shipping:** Handled by dedicated product teams and IT for platform delivery, with content creation involving both internal and external experts.
- **Sales-input:** The company actively engages with potential students, including direct sales calls, indicating a strong sales-input loop.
- **Design:** Content design focuses on conciseness, practicality, and engagement, with a methodology of breaking down complex topics into short, digestible lessons.

## Evidence
- *The Power MBA said I'm going to remove all the costs, I don't have a campus, I don't have in-person students.*
- *We have a lot of programs that, evidently, I don't create them because I have a limit of knowledge and time.*

## Gaps
- The specific metrics used to evaluate program success beyond sales figures are not detailed.
- The internal processes for quality control and curriculum updates across all faculties are not fully elaborated.
- The long-term strategy for maintaining competitive differentiation in a rapidly evolving online education market is not deeply explored.
