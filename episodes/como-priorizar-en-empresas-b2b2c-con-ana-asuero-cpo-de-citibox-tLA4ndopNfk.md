## Thesis
In B2B2C, Citibox prioritizes carrier ROI and building-level installation economics over end-user feature polish — the mailbox only wins if logistics partners adopt it.


## Context
Citibox provides a service that installs smart mailboxes in residential buildings to streamline package delivery and returns. The company operates in the B2C2B space, where they need to convince building administrators and residents to adopt their solution, while also partnering with numerous courier companies to utilize their network.

## Operating loop
Citibox's operational loop involves:
- Identifying suitable buildings based on size, location, and historical delivery data.
- Gaining approval from building administrators or community presidents for installation.
- Installing smart mailboxes, often with physical activation materials.
- Encouraging residents (end-users) to register and use the service for package reception and returns.
- Partnering with courier companies to integrate Citibox into their delivery routes.
- Continuously improving the hardware and software based on user and carrier feedback.

## Ownership
- **Discovery**: Primarily driven by user feedback from residents and couriers, with a focus on new building installations and expanding use cases.
- **Prioritization**: Balances the needs of end-users (residents) and business users (couriers and building administrators), with a focus on metrics related to user acquisition and activation.
- **Shipping**: Managed by two product squads, one focused on hardware network maintenance and user acquisition/activation, and the other on user engagement and growth post-first package pickup.
- **Sales-input**: Influenced by both end-user requests for installation and courier needs for operational efficiency.
- **Design**: Involves both physical mailbox design and digital interfaces for users and couriers, with options for some physical customization.
